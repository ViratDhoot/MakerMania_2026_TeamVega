## CAD, Laser Cutting and 3D Printing

Through CAD practice, we worked on basic 3D modelling, dimensions, shape creation, and exporting files for fabrication.

Some practice outputs included:

* Toy block
* Bottle models
* Doorstop
* Hex nut
* Paper clip
* Keychain models
* Microphone model
* Stuffing box components

Laser cutting helped us understand clean 2D design, correct file preparation, scale, and MDF cutting.

3D printing helped us understand that a model should not only look correct digitally, but should also be printable. Orientation, supports, and print setup played an important role.

# Week 1 Highlight: BusyBuds

The main Week 1 activity was the **Useless Product Challenge**.

Our product was **BusyBuds** — fake solid earbuds that do not play sound, connect to Bluetooth, or need charging. The product is technically useless, but it was pitched as a social signal to look busy and avoid interruptions.

## Why the Useless Product Activity Helped

This activity pushed us to think beyond obvious useful products. Instead of starting with a normal problem-solution approach, we had to explore unusual ideas and still make them convincing.

BusyBuds helped us understand that product thinking is also about:

* Framing an idea clearly
* Understanding user behaviour
* Making an idea relatable
* Using humour and storytelling
* Presenting even unusual concepts convincingly

The activity helped us think broadly and consider multiple directions before locking an idea.

## Key Learnings

* CAD and fabrication require practical thinking, not just digital modelling.
* 3D printing depends heavily on orientation, supports, and print setup.
* Laser cutting needs clean outlines, correct scale, and proper file preparation.
* Product pitching depends on framing, storytelling, and clarity.
* The useless product task helped us think broadly and explore unusual directions.
* Documentation is easier when it is updated continuously.
