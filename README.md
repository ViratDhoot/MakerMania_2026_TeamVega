## 1.4 Week 2 Update: Problem Discovery, SCAMPER & ESP32 Revision

Week 2 started with problem discussions, user research, ideation activities, and basic electronics revision.

### Activities Covered

* Reviewed Google Form responses and informal problem inputs
* Discussed multiple possible problem statements
* Evaluated ideas based on user pain, feasibility, cost, and hardware scope
* Performed a SCAMPER-based ideation activity
* Revised ESP32 basics through a simple LED series blink activity

### SCAMPER Activity Summary

During the SCAMPER activity, we discussed multiple ideas and tried to evaluate them from different angles.

One idea discussed by Virat was a local-train bag holder for people who experience shoulder pain while carrying heavy bags. The concept involved a hook-like or 3D printed support mechanism. However, we dropped it because the load was mostly shifted rather than reduced, and the problem framing was not clear enough at that stage.

Another idea discussed was a low-cost safety device for delivery and gig workers who work in changing weather and traffic conditions. The idea was to track basic safety and health indicators such as heat exposure, accident possibility, heart rate, SpO2, and outdoor working conditions. This idea was selected for the SCAMPER activity and will be discussed further, but it has not been finalized as the main project problem statement.

Activity file: [SCAMPER Activity PPT](Training%20Material/Week%202%20-%20SCAMPER%20Activity/SCAMPER%20Activity%20-%20Team%20Vega.pptx)

# 14. Reflection

## What Worked Well?

Week 1:

* Team completed the useless product activity.
* BusyBuds was taken from an idea to a 3D printed product and advertisement.
* We got hands-on exposure to CAD, laser cutting, 3D printing, documentation, and video-based pitching.
* The useless product task helped us think broadly and frame even unusual ideas convincingly.

Week 2 so far:

* Problem discovery has started with Google Form responses and informal discussions.
* Multiple ideas were discussed before narrowing down.
* The SCAMPER activity helped compare ideas from different perspectives.
* ESP32 basics were revised through a simple hardware activity.

---

## What Failed?

Week 1:

* The first 3D print attempt for BusyBuds needed improvement because the support setup was not planned well.
* Some decisions took time to finalize during team activities.
* Documentation could have been updated more frequently during the activity.

Week 2 so far:

* Some early ideas were too vague or difficult to frame clearly.
* The local-train bag holder idea was dropped because it shifted the load instead of reducing it.
* Some high-impact ideas were too expensive, AI-heavy, or difficult to prototype within the current scope.

---

## Key Learnings

Week 1:

* CAD and 3D printing require attention to dimensions, supports, orientation, and printability.
* A product pitch depends not only on the object, but also on framing, storytelling, and presentation.
* The useless product activity helped us explore multiple directions before locking an idea.
* Documentation is easier when progress is recorded continuously.

Week 2 so far:

* A good problem statement needs a clear user, pain point, and use case.
* Not every relatable problem becomes a strong prototype idea.
* Hardware ideas need to be checked for cost, feasibility, mounting, durability, and user value.
* SCAMPER helps in expanding ideas, but the final project still needs deeper validation.
* Even basic ESP32 revision is useful for getting back into the embedded workflow.

---

## Next Steps

* Continue collecting and shortlisting problem statements
* Conduct user interviews / informal discussions
* Select a strong final problem statement
* Start solution ideation
* Begin technical planning and early prototyping
* Keep updating the repository with progress, notes, photos, videos, and learnings
